Build your own line

A step-by-step guide and a setup script you can read before you run it turn an Ubuntu cloud server you control into a server your iPhone trusts. It ends with a QR code you scan into VolaVPN.

Proof run

Before you rely on a line, VolaVPN resolves the address, dials it, checks that your exit address changed and holds the connection for 10 seconds — each step with a real timestamp.

Share by QR code

Share a line you run as a QR code or as a configuration profile, without its password by default. Including the password asks you to confirm and to unlock with Face ID or your passcode first.

Also in the app

  • Plain-English failures. When a line fails, VolaVPN shows what went wrong where it can tell — an address that doesn't resolve, a server that doesn't answer, an expired certificate — and what to check next.
  • Auto-join rules. Connect on cellular, or on Wi-Fi except the networks you list. You type the network names, so VolaVPN doesn't need your location.
  • Your lines, your names. Add several servers, name and rename them, and keep them in the order you like. Import a line by scanning its QR code, choosing a QR image, pasting its link or typing it in.
  • Logbook. Every session with its start, end, duration and why it ended. VolaVPN doesn't show bytes sent or received — iOS doesn't provide that to apps like this one.
  • Starter line. Open for 2 hours from the first time you open VolaVPN. When it closes, everything else keeps working — there's nothing to buy and no account.

What your server needs

A server iOS can connect to natively with username and password (EAP) sign-in, such as strongSwan, with a certificate iPhone already trusts, such as one from Let's Encrypt. OpenVPN, WireGuard and L2TP servers are not supported. The full list · Build one with the setup script

VolaVPN doesn't provide, rent or manage servers. You choose a cloud provider and pay them directly, if at all. No account, no analytics, no ads.
